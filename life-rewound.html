Conway's Game of Life only runs forwards. This runs it backwards: draw a pattern as it exists now, and every configuration that would have produced it one tick later gets counted — exactly, not sampled — and laid out for you to page through.

A past here is any configuration confined to the search window that becomes your drawing exactly — every live cell in place, and nothing alive anywhere else on the plane. The window matters: on an unbounded board you could always park a lone cell a mile away, watch it die of loneliness, and call that a different past, so the count would be infinite. Widen the window and the count grows; a pattern with none at all is a Garden of Eden. Counting uses an exact row-by-row transfer DP over B3/S23, so the totals are complete, not estimated, and the browser lists the n-th past without ever building the other n−1.
