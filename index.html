SOURCES

Ephemeris is a reference atlas, not a precision simulator. Positions are computed from published orbital elements; outside 1800–2050, and far from each element set’s epoch, they become approximate.

Orbits and positions

  • Planets: JPL “Keplerian elements for approximate positions of the major planets” (E. M. Standish).
  • All 461 planetary satellites: JPL Solar System Dynamics planetary satellite mean elements.
  • 1,581,258 asteroids, 4,088 comets and every featured small body, with discovery records and naming citations: JPL Small-Body Database.
  • Moons of dwarf planets and asteroids, sizes and rings: the Celestia catalogue (CelestiaProject/CelestiaContent, GPL-2.0-or-later).
  • Space stations and Hubble: CelesTrak two-line elements. Tesla Roadster: JPL Horizons.
  • Stars: HYG stellar database v4.1 by David Nash (astronexus), CC BY-SA 4.0.

Imagery and shape models

Surface maps are mosaics of spacecraft photographs from NASA, ESA, JAXA and USGS missions, assembled by the Celestia community (Askaniy Anpilogov, ItzImcool, Björn Jónsson, Paul Schenk, Phil Stooke, John van Vliet, Ted Stryk and others) under CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC BY-NC-SA and CC0 licences — the info panel credits each map. Shape models come from NASA PDS, ESA, JAXA and radar teams; the ISS and Mir models are by NASA and DaveBowman2001. The Milky Way panorama is from Solar System Scope (CC BY 4.0). Objects never photographed up close use representative textures, and the panel says so.
