Press record and make some noise — up to 10 seconds.

1 · The sound

Your microphone captures air pressure over time as a list of numbers between −1 and 1. That list, drawn left to right, is the amber wave above.

2 · The anti-sound

Multiply every number by −1 and you get the cyan wave: a perfect mirror. On its own it sounds identical to the original — your ear can’t hear absolute polarity.

3 · The zero

Play both at once and each sample adds to its own negative: x + (−x) = 0. The speaker cone never moves. This is the same trick noise-cancelling headphones pull off in real time.

4 · Break it with time

Silence only works if the mirror is perfectly on time. Delay it even a few milliseconds and each sample meets the wrong partner — some frequencies cancel, others double. That hollow, phasey sound is called comb filtering.
